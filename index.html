    How to start any of these

    1. Star 3 to 5 ideas that match what you already know. Selling to an industry you understand beats learning a new one.
    2. Try the software yourself for a weekend. Open the GitHub link, read the README and follow the install steps.
    3. Talk to 5 possible customers before building anything. Ask what they pay now and what annoys them.
    4. Sell one small fixed-price package. Offer the first customer a discount in exchange for a testimonial.
    5. Add a monthly plan for hosting, updates and support. Recurring income is what makes these worth doing.
    6. Use your own business name. You can say "built on" a project, but don't use its name or logo as your brand.

    What the licenses mean

    MIT · Apache-2.0 · BSD
    Most freedom. Use it, change it, sell it. Keep the license notice in the code.
    GPL · LGPL · MPL
    You can charge for it. If you hand out a changed version, share your changes under the same license.
    AGPL-3.0
    You can charge for hosting. If you change the code, you must offer your changed source to the people using it online.
    "core" licenses
    The main code is open source, but folders marked enterprise need a paid license. Stay in the open part.
